Interpret the whole conversation before producing the final structured record. Later corrections, answers, refusals and acceptances update the meaning of earlier speech. The examples below illustrate general distinctions; they are not evidence about the supplied meeting.

Evidence fidelity applies to EVERY field, including questions, rationales, participant lists, risks, ticket titles and descriptions. A valid citation ID is not enough: its content must support the actual claim. Preserve conditions, negation, uncertainty, tense and scope. Do not add a plausible cause, purpose, deadline, agreement or owner. A question can contain an unsupported assertion: if a module is only suspected, ask whether it is involved rather than asking why its defect caused the incident.

Ownership and stance are separate from speaking. Someone reading a document or announcing a collective decision is not thereby the task owner, a personal supporter, or an objector. Populate individual stance lists only when that person's own position is evidenced. A request, guess about identity, quoted assignment, or confident assertion by another person is not acceptance. Preserve a definitely heard commitment even when the human identity is unknown, using a null owner and one targeted clarification; never use an unidentified-speaker placeholder as an identified owner. Keep separately accepted work by known people.

Distinguish proposals from accepted work. An explicitly conditional offer may remain a candidate with its conditions and no invented deadline; it is not a confirmed commitment. Preserve positive commitments when they are made, including later acceptance of a previously conditional offer. Preserve superseded decisions as history alongside the current decision. Completed historical work and hypothetical examples are not new commissions. 'No decision that X is needed' does not mean 'X is unnecessary'.

Open questions must remain materially unresolved at the end. Remove an earlier question once later speech answers it. A future decision can remain open even when the present state is known: 'Will approval be granted?' differs from asking whether approval has already happened after the source explicitly says it has not. Consolidate equivalent questions; preserve distinct unanswered issues. Do not suppress real tasks, explicit personal objections, material risks, or unresolved questions merely to avoid errors.

Respect accepted scope. An investigation is not a repair commitment. Accepting one component of a request does not accept all components. A discussion explaining a deadline does not define the subject of a test. Follow-up titles must express the same supported work as the action; no new purpose or assignment may be introduced when shortening it. Output compact descriptions without redundant copies of every utterance.

Use the supplied source instant and Europe/Berlin calendar for relative dates, including midnight and daylight-saving boundaries. Keep the source phrase, distinguish a wish date from an accepted deadline, and attach dates only to the work they qualify. Do not use the current machine date. Where the source resolves a date or scope question, do not reopen it.

Illustrative contrasts:
- 'The timeout could be in router.ts or the upstream service' supports investigating both possibilities, not 'Why did router.ts fail?'.
- 'The committee replaced option Red with Blue; I am reporting the result' supports a decision revision, not a personal objection by the messenger.
- 'Is Friday firm?' / 'Yes, I accept delivery Friday' settles that deadline question. 'The launch is still unapproved' does not unsettle the accepted documentation deadline.
- 'I can investigate if we fund it' is conditional; 'Funding is confirmed; I now accept the investigation' is a commitment. Do not treat both statements as two tasks.
- 'We discussed the holiday to calculate when the serializer review is due' does not make the review a holiday-behavior test.

Before returning JSON, check that every assertion is supported, all later updates are reconciled, every materially accepted task and unresolved question is retained, and no duplicate work or invented scope was added. Keep this check internal; return only the requested JSON.


Retain a material unresolved future choice even when the source definitively states that no current task, approval or commission exists. 'Nobody has decided whether we need this' leaves the need unresolved; 'we decided this is unnecessary' resolves it. An answer can therefore correctly have no tasks and still have an open question. Apply uncertainty to short titles too: investigating whether a component caused a problem differs from investigating a known defect in that component. Before returning, scan for unanswered needs and for titles that accidentally assert an unverified cause.
